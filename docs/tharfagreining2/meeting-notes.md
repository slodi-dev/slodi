### #6 — Beinagrind first (fixed slots), then puzzle content in ⭐ key

> "Beinagrind sett saman: Það fyrsta sem ég geri er að setja saman beinagrindina
> fyrir skátafundinn — setning, dagskrá, leikur, slit og endurmat — svo púsla ég
> saman mismunandi hlutum inn í það. Vanalega er ég með einhverja eina setningu
> eða slit sem ég keyri í mánuð og svo rótera, þannig það er alltaf eins, svo vinn
> ég út frá þemunum sem við erum búin að vinna. Oftast gef ég skátunum þrjú
> dagskrárþemu sem þau geta unnið út frá og ákveðið, og svo fá krakkarnir að
> skipuleggja fundi út frá því og það fer niður á…"

**Interpretation — direct hit on the §5.1 model test for a skátafundur.**
The workflow, in order:
1. **Assemble a beinagrind (skeleton) first** — a **fixed, named slot structure**:
   **setning → dagskrá → leikur → slit → endurmat.** (opening → program →
   game → closing → reflection/evaluation.)
2. **"Púsla saman mismunandi hlutum inn í það"** — then *puzzle different parts
   into* the skeleton. This **is the assemble/drag-and-drop action** the Planner
   is built around.
3. **Reusable structural elements with a rotation cadence:** one setning/slit runs
   **for a month**, then rotates — "alltaf eins." Structural slots have their own
   reuse rhythm.
4. **Theme-driven content** ("vinn út frá þemunum") — cf. #4, #5.
5. **Youth choice from a constrained set:** leader offers **three dagskrárþemu**;
   kids pick and **plan the meetings** from there. Refines #4 — not fully open;
   the leader **frames with ~3 options**.

- **Validates (strongly):**
  - **`Event → ordered Tasks` fits** — the meeting *is* a sequence of named
    slots; the leader spontaneously listed them. Answers §5.1 "is there a fixed
    structure? where do opening/closing fit?" → **yes, explicitly.**
  - **The Planner solves a real problem** — "assemble a skeleton, then fill it"
    is literally the product. **Moves the §1 kill/pivot criterion to the
    _keep-building_ side** for this leader: their mental model *is* assembling
    ordered activities.
  - **Templates (§5.3):** the beinagrind is a **structural template**; setning /
    slit are **reusable elements** with a monthly rotation → argues for
    element-level reuse, not just whole-meeting copies.
- **New model nuance:** slots like **setning / slit / endurmat** aren't generic
  Tasks — they're **typed structural roles**. Question for the model: are these
  a `Task.role`/category, or fixed sections of an `Event` template?

### #8 — Youth-led theme process + a template *for the scouts* to plan with

> "Ræði við skátana um hvernig þema þau vilja hafa í dagskrá. Skátarnir kynna
> mismunandi þema og svo er kosið. Skátarnir skipuleggja næstu fundi út frá þessu
> þema með aðstoð foringja. Gott að láta skátana fá template um hvernig fundurinn
> á að vera og hvað þau þurfa."

**Interpretation:** a defined **democratic, youth-led** planning process —
1. **discuss** themes with the scouts → 2. scouts **present** different themes →
3. **vote** → 4. scouts **plan the next meetings** from the chosen theme **with
leader help.** Plus a concrete ask: **give the scouts a template** for *how the
meeting should be* and *what they need*.

- **Reinforces:** #4 (theme-first, kids fill), #5 (purposeful), #6 (beinagrind +
  3-options→pick). #8 adds the **voting/democratic** step and makes the
  youth-led process explicit and repeatable.
- **Key new distinction — template as scaffolding, not just time-saving:**
  the template here is **youth-facing** — it teaches kids *how to plan a meeting*
  (the beinagrind from #6) **and** "hvað þau þurfa" (the **gear/materials**
  dimension, §5.1 "with what gear"). This is a *different purpose* from #2's
  reuse-to-save-time: it's a **teaching/enablement** tool. Templates serve **two
  jobs** — (a) leader productivity, (b) scaffolding youth. Don't conflate them.
- **Collaboration (§5.2):** strongest evidence yet that **scouts are real
  participants** in planning — they need (guided) access to a planning template,

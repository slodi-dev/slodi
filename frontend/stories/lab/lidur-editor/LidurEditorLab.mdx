# LidurEditor: usage and directions

LidurEditor is the editor for one liður on a fundur sheet. This page covers when a leader opens
it, which edits are common and which are rare, and what must never be lost. Then it explains
the four directions in this lab and when each one fits. The stories sit beside this page:
**Comparison** first, then eight stories per direction, then **Remove, then Afturkalla** and
**Playground**.

The sheet the editors open from is a plain stand-in (time, heiti, staða, lengd). The real sheet
is being redesigned in **Lab → FundurSheet**. The editors don't depend on how the row looks.
All they need from it is a button to open from and to give focus back to. They use the same
staða glyphs as that lab (✓ Staðfest, ✎ Drög, ~ Óvíst, ? Óákveðið).

## 1. Usage note

### When a leader opens the editor

- **While planning, on a laptop.** Usually a Sunday evening, building next week's fundur. The
  leader goes down the sheet adjusting one liður after another. This is most of the editing,
  and it comes in runs of several liðir.
- **Right after a block lands from the bank.** Its name is generic ("Leikur"), its lengd is a
  default, and its staða is Óákveðið. The leader renames it, sets the lengd and moves the staða
  on.
- **At the fundur, on a phone.** It's raining, so the game is cut short. One quick change, one
  hand, a noisy room.

### Frequent edits and rare ones

| Frequent (every planning session)                          | Rare (a few times a term)                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| **Lengd**, almost always ±5 or ±10 minutes                 | **Færa í fund…**: "we did this too late"     |
| **Staða**: Óákveðið → Drög → Staðfest as the plan firms up | **Afrita**: the same game twice              |
| **Heiti**: a block from the bank gets its real name        | **Tegund**: which section it belongs in      |
|                                                            | **Staðsetning**: only when it goes elsewhere |

The rare fields stay reachable in every direction. They just don't take up first-glance space.

### What must never be lost

- **Typed text survives every way of closing except an explicit "Henda breytingum".** Today's
  editor writes every keystroke straight through. That keeps text, but it means nothing can be
  discarded, so Escape can't be a way to back out. Every direction here holds a **draft**:
  - **Lokið** saves.
  - In B, **moving to another liður** saves too, and says so.
  - **Færa í fund…** and **Afrita** carry the draft with them.
- **Escape closes.** So do ✕ and the backdrop (B on a phone, and C). If something changed, rule
  6's question comes first: "Henda breytingum?". It names what changed ("Þú breyttir lengd og
  stöðu á „Áttavitinn: undirstöður“"). The quiet choice, **Halda áfram að breyta**, comes first
  and has focus, so a second Escape or Enter loses nothing. **Henda breytingum** is red.
  Escape on the question closes only the question (rule 7, topmost layer).
- **An empty heiti doesn't save.** Lokið says "Gefðu liðnum heiti." under the field and puts
  focus there (rule 6).
- **Enter in a field never submits.** There is no `<form>`. Enter in Heiti and Staðsetning does
  nothing, and Enter in the lengd number commits the typed value. Staða is a radio group, so
  Enter has nothing to submit.

### Endurmat from last time

Endurmat is the reason to reuse a block, so it appears **while editing, above the fields it is
about**. It isn't a link to follow. It shows:

- the note in quotes;
- **who wrote it and when** ("Signý Hrund · 23. september 2026", through `formatIcelandicDate`),
  because a note from last week weighs more than one from two years ago;
- when the note is about length, **one tap to act on it** ("Setja lengd í 20 mín"). That's the
  commonest reason to read it at all.

It is an inline note (`role="note"`) in the info colour. It isn't Fálkar red, the endurmat kind
colour, because red reads as an error in this palette. In D, where lengd is edited on the row,
the note sits under the row itself.

### Removing a liður

"Fjarlægja lið" is **reversible**, so under rule 5 it happens at once. There's no confirm dialog:

- The editor closes and a toast says "Liður fjarlægður: Kortalestur í hverfinu." with
  **Afturkalla** for 10 seconds (rule 5 asks for at least 8).
- Focus goes to the next row.
- Afturkalla puts the liður back in its place, with focus on it.

A confirm dialog would be the wrong tool. It asks "are you sure?" about something that costs
nothing to undo, and leaders learn to click through it, so it fails on the day it matters.
"Færa í fund…" works the same way. "Fjarlægja lið" is a quiet underlined text button, last in
the row, so it's hard to hit by accident without being hidden.

### What every direction does

- **Labels.** Every field has a visible label. Lengd and staða are labelled groups (`fieldset`
  and `legend` for staða), and every icon button has a name.
- **Focus.** Opening from the row puts focus on the first field, Heiti. Closing returns it to
  the row's button. B is the exception: a click on another row moves the panel and leaves focus
  on that row, so you can keep going down the sheet.
- **Size.** Inputs are 16px (`--sl-text-body`), so iOS doesn't zoom. Targets are 44px, and 48px
  on touch and below 620px.
- **Themes.** Light, dark and patrol work from `--sl-*` tokens alone, checked by axe in every
  theme.
- **Text.** `countIs` ("5 liðir", "20 mínútur"), `formatMinutes` and `formatIcelandicDate`.
- **Motion.** The editor fades in and the sheet rises. Both go under `prefers-reduced-motion`, and
  the transition tokens collapse to 0 there as well.

## 2. The directions

### A · Inline

**The story:** "Fix this one, here, without losing your place." It's today's editor, refined.

**Hierarchy:**

1. The endurmat, when there is one.
2. Heiti.
3. Lengd and staða side by side on one line, staða as a four-way segmented control.
4. "Meira", which says what it holds ("tegund, staðsetning, færa, afrita"), and Lokið.

**Why it looks this way:**

- It opens under its row, inside the same list item, so "liður 3 af 5" stays true. The leader is
  thinking about the liðir on either side, and a panel at the edge of the window breaks that.
- Today's editor gives Tegund and Staðsetning the same weight as Lengd. That makes four boxes of
  equal importance. Here the frequent three come first, and the rest is one click away.
- Staða is four radios drawn as one control: one Tab stop, arrows to change. The old
  `aria-pressed` chips were four Tab stops.

**When to use it:** the default for the bench on a laptop, and fine on a phone, where the frequent
fields stack.

**Trade-offs:**

- It pushes the rows below it down. Editing several liðir means open, close, open.
- Only one liður is open at a time.
- "Meira" hides the move-to-fundur select, which is the only way to move between fundir.

### B · Side inspector

**The story:** "Go down the evening, one liður after another." A properties panel, as in a design
tool.

**Hierarchy:**

1. A header with the liður's name, its time and place in the fundur, and ‹ › to step through the
   fundur.
2. Endurmat.
3. Heiti, lengd, staða (2×2).
4. A rule, then tegund, staðsetning and færa, all visible because the panel has the room.
5. Lokið, Afrita and Fjarlægja.

**Why it looks this way:**

- Planning is a pass down the whole sheet. A panel that stays open while you click liðir (or
  press ‹ ›) turns ten open-and-close cycles into ten clicks.
- What you typed travels with you. Moving to another liður saves the draft and announces
  "Breytingar vistaðar: …". The panel never asks, because nothing has been lost.
- The sheet doesn't move while you edit, so the running time stays in view.
- Below 900px there's no room beside the sheet, so the panel becomes a modal bottom sheet. That
  follows rule 7: Tab stays inside, the page doesn't scroll, and Escape and the backdrop ask
  first.

**When to use it:** laptop planning sessions, which is where most editing happens.

**Trade-offs:**

- It costs 20–24rem of width that the sheet and the bank also want. With the bank rail open, the
  sheet gets narrow.
- The edited liður and its row are apart, so the row is highlighted to tie them together.
- On a phone it is just C with fewer niceties.

### C · Bottom sheet

**The story:** "One thumb, at the fundur." Built for the phone first.

**Hierarchy:**

1. A grabber and the liður's name as a heading.
2. Endurmat with its one-tap lengd.
3. Heiti.
4. Lengd as five big chips (5, 10, 15, 20, 30), with a 56px stepper under them for anything
   else.
5. Staða as a 2×2 segmented control.
6. "Meira".
7. A sticky footer with a full-width **Lokið**, and Fjarlægja lið on the far side.

**Why it looks this way:**

- In a loud room you tap a number, you don't type one. The chips cover nearly every lengd a
  fundur uses, and the stepper covers the rest. The design-system TimingField pairs presets with
  a stepper in the same way.
- Every target is 48px or more. Lokið is the biggest thing in the sheet and sits where the thumb
  rests.
- It respects the iPhone safe area and uses `dvh`. It's a real modal layer: Tab is trapped,
  scroll is locked, and the backdrop and Escape ask first (rule 7).
- On a laptop the same content opens as a centred dialog.

**When to use it:** at the fundur, and for any touch-first planning.

**Trade-offs:**

- It covers the sheet, so you can't see the running time change as you edit.
- On a laptop it's heavier than A or B for a one-field change.
- The chips duplicate the stepper. That's deliberate, but it's one more way to do the same thing.

### D · Quick edit

**The story:** "Most edits aren't edits." The frequent changes need no form at all.

**Hierarchy, on the row:**

1. Time and title.
2. **− lengd +** and a **staða menu** right on the row.
3. The endurmat note under the row, with its one-tap lengd.

The full editor holds only Heiti, Tegund, Staðsetning, Færa, Afrita and Fjarlægja.

**Why it looks this way:**

- Lengd and staða are single values with an obvious next step. A form round-trip (open, change,
  Lokið, close) for "+5" is four actions for one change. Here it's one.
- They write straight through, and each change is announced ("Lengd 20 mínútur: Kötturinn og
  músin."). A single tap is its own undo, so there's no discard question for them.
- The staða menu is a real menu (`menuitemradio`): arrows to move, Enter to pick, Escape to close
  without changes. Focus goes back to the pill either way.
- The editor, which handles the rare fields, keeps the draft and the discard question.

**When to use it:** together with A or B, as the row's own controls. It doesn't replace an
editor.

**Trade-offs:**

- Every row carries six more controls, so the sheet gets busy. On a phone they need a second line
  per row, and the sheet gets much taller.
- The real row already has ▲ ▼ ✎ ✕ and a grip. Adding − + and a menu is a lot of row.
- Two ways to save: straight through on the row, a draft in the editor. That's consistent per
  field, but it has to be explained once.

## 3. Choosing

- **A + D's staða menu** is the smallest step from today. It's the inline editor, with the one
  change leaders make most, staða, taken out of the form.
- **B** is worth it only if the bench gets wide enough to give up 22rem. Check that against the
  fundur-sheet lab's directions.
- **C** should be what every direction becomes below 620px. B already does this, and A could too.

## 4. Before promoting any of these

- **The shared `Modal` isn't good enough for B or C.** It doesn't trap Tab or restore focus, and
  its Escape listener on `document` would also close the editor under it. It confirms with
  `window.confirm`, which rule 5 forbids. The lab carries a stand-in (`useModalLayer`,
  `DiscardQuestion`). Promoting a modal direction means fixing `Modal` first.
- **The API doesn't carry endurmat's author and date yet.** `Lidur.endurmat` is a plain string.
  The lab's `LabEndurmat` (`text`, `by`, `at`, `suggestMinutes`) is the shape these designs need.
- **A draft instead of write-through** changes `benchState`. The editor would dispatch one
  `patch` on Lokið rather than one per keystroke. The reducer already supports that.

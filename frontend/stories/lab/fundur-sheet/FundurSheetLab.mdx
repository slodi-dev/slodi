# FundurSheet and LidurRow: the current form, restyled

The fundur sheet is the most important surface in the Vinnubekkur: one fundur, its three bands and
its liðir in order. The review kept **the current sheet's form**: its functions and interactions are
right. This lab fixes what's wrong with how it looks, and tries four styles on top of the fix.

Every style is live. It renders from the real `BenchProvider` and reducer, so ↑ ↓, the drag
handle, Space / arrow keys / Enter / Delete, "Skipta á flokka" and "Sameina í eitt", the inline
`LidurEditor` and the announcements behave exactly as on the bench, and the split bands use the
real `SplitGrid`. The keyboard handler and the duration nudge in `LidurRowLab.tsx` are copied
unchanged from `LidurRow.tsx`. Stories: **Lab → FundurSheet → Styles**. _Comparison_ shows the
current sheet first, then F0–F4 on the same over-time fundur.

## Who reads it, and when

- **A sveitarforingi on a laptop on Sunday evening**, building next Wednesday's fundur: pulling
  blocks from the bank, moving things until the evening fits, changing lengths, splitting
  Verkefni fundar by flokkur.
- **The same person on a phone, at the fundur**, in a loud room, glancing between activities:
  _what's next, when does it start, are we late?_ They rarely edit, and when they do it's a quick
  move ("the game goes first, it's raining").

So the sheet has to read in a glance (times and names), say whether the evening fits without
scrolling to the bottom, show what still needs a decision, and keep every control reachable with a
thumb.

## What's wrong with the current sheet

Fixed in **every** style, F0 included.

1. **The kind is a coloured stripe nobody can decode.** Yellow, green, blue and teal 3px bars by
   liður kind, with no legend; the kind itself is screen-reader only. It's the generic "accent
   stripe" look, and it competes with the status colours. → The stripe is gone. The kind is a
   word, shown only when it differs from the band's own kind (a _Leikur_ in Verkefni fundar, not
   _Setning_ under Setning).
2. **Whether it fits is only at the bottom.** The header says "19:30–21:10" and nothing tells you
   that's ten minutes late. → The header says "áætlað 90 mín (til 21:00)" and a "10 mín yfir"
   chip (or "Passar", or "10 mín laus").
3. **The title shouts.** A 24px uppercase title, and a filled green pill for the theme, are the two
   loudest things on the sheet; the liðir are what you read. → Sentence-case title with the date
   beside it in secondary weight; the theme is an outlined chip.
4. **Rows aren't as tall as their time.** The row height is meant to show duration, but at 1.5px a
   minute with a 52px floor, 10, 15, 30 and 35 minutes all draw 52–53px tall. → 2.4px a minute
   with a 48px floor: 10 → 48, 30 → 72, 35 → 84.
5. **It reads like a spreadsheet.** Vertical rules between the handle, the clock and the title make
   every row three boxes. → One hairline under each row; columns by alignment, not rules.
6. **Every status looks alike.** Staðfest and Drög are the same outlined uppercase pill. → What
   needs the leader is tinted (Óvíst on its warning tint, Drög outlined in info); Staðfest is plain
   green text; Óákveðið stays dashed, never red.
7. **"Skipta á flokka" is three pills.** An outlined chip on every band is the most repeated object
   on the sheet. → A quiet text button with a columns icon, and the band's minutes sit next to its
   name rather than at the far edge in tertiary grey.
8. **Small, glyph buttons.** ↑ ↓ ✎ ✕ and ⠿ are text glyphs in 28px boxes. → lucide icons in 36px
   buttons, 48px on touch or a narrow sheet (UX rule 8). The duration button looks like a button on
   hover and has a real hit area.
9. **The endurmat hides in a tooltip.** "Endurmat frá síðast" is a label with the note in `title`,
   which touch and screen readers never see. → The note is shown, two lines at most.
10. **The footer contradicts the rows.** "ALLIR LIÐIR ÁKVEÐNIR" in grey uppercase while two liðir
    are Drög. → Sentence case, warning-coloured while something is undecided, and "· 2 í drögum"
    when there are drafts.
11. **An undated fundur reads "—" on every row.** → "+0:10", "+0:40": time since the start.
12. **Phone.** The four buttons took 40% of the width, so every title wrapped to two lines and the
    sub-line broke around its dots. → On a narrow sheet (a container query, so a narrow desktop
    column behaves the same), the buttons share the clock's line and the title gets the full width.
13. **Generic card.** Border, radius and a shadow that grows on the active sheet. → The active sheet
    is marked by a stronger edge; no shadows.

## The styles

### F0 · Current, fixed up

**Story:** the sheet you know, without its faults. **Hierarchy:** title → clock and fit → liðir
(clock, name) → status → controls. **Why:** to judge the styles against something, and because
it may be all we need. **Trade-offs:** still a card in a card-shaped app; nothing about it says
"skátar".

### F1 · Quiet

**Story:** a programme, not a form. **Hierarchy:** the clock (heading size) and the name (body-lg)
carry the page; everything else is secondary text. No card, no band fills: a strong rule on top,
hairlines between rows, band names as small headings. The status is a dot and a word (filled when
staðfest, a ring for Drög, a dashed ring for Óákveðið). The handle shows on hover and focus, and
always on touch. **Why:** the fastest to read at the fundur, on a phone in a loud room.
**Trade-offs:** without a frame, two sheets in a long bench run together; the past/active states
rely on the top rule alone. The quietest status is also the easiest to miss on Sunday evening.

### F2 · Stamped

**Story:** the fundur is a page in the season's passport, and each liður gets stamped as it's
decided. The calendar's own stamps (`components/plan/calendar/Stamp.tsx`, the same mild ink) sit
before every name: inked when staðfest, dashed for Drög, dotted with a "?" for Óvíst and an empty
dotted box for Óákveðið. The band names are ink labels (a double rule, a slight tilt), the date is
a stamped box. **Hierarchy:** stamp and name → clock → status word (in the stamp's ink, no pill).
**Why:** one visual family with the Dagatal (D4), PlanGrid A5 and SplitGrid S2, so "ready" looks
the same wherever you see it. The stamp also makes the undecided liðir pop out of a column of
inked ones. **Trade-offs:** the most decorated; the double frame and tilted labels cost a little
space on a phone; the stamp's picture comes from the theme, so liðir in one fundur often share it.

### F3 · Run sheet

**Story:** the paper the foringi holds at the fundur. Ruled lines, a red margin rule after the
clock, typewriter (mono) times and minutes, a tick box per liður and the status in italic, like a
pencilled note. Printing hides the handle, the buttons and "Skipta á flokka" and keeps rows from
breaking across pages. **Hierarchy:** time → tick box and name → note. **Why:** many fundir are
still run from a printout or a phone held like one; this is the style that's honest about it.
**Trade-offs:** the tick box is decorative (ticking off during the fundur isn't in the model);
mono numbers take more width; it looks less like an app, which some will like and some won't.

### F4 · Patrol colour

**Story:** whose evening is it? The header wears the flokkur's colour (Ernir yellow, Refir blue…)
with the flokkur's name, and the clock column and band rows carry its tint. A troop-wide fundur
wears the sveit's green. The patrol colour is a background behind its own `-foreground` text, and
the tint only sits under primary text, so both themes keep AA; the fit chip sits on the surface.
**Why:** in a bench with four flokkar's fundir in a row, colour finds your own before you read.
**Trade-offs:** it needs the fundur's flokkur, which the API doesn't give (below); a sheet that is
mostly yellow is loud; status colours on a patrol tint are harder to pick out.

## What it needs from the backend

Nothing for F0–F3. **F4** needs the fundur's flokkur: `Fundur` has `scope` but no `patrol_id`, so
the lab reads it off the fixture title ("Flokksfundur Arna" → Ernir), which only works for the
fixtures.

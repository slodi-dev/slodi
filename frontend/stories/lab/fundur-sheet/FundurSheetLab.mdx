# FundurSheet and LidurRow: usage and directions

The fundur sheet is the most important surface in the Vinnubekkur: one fundur, its three sections
and its liðir in order. This page works out who reads it, when, and what they need to see first.
Then it explains the four directions in **Lab → FundurSheet → Directions**, and when each one
fits.

All four directions are live. They render from the real `BenchProvider` and reducer
(`benchState.ts`), so ▲ ▼, the drag handle, Space / arrow keys / Enter / Delete, "Skipta á
flokka", the inline editor and the announcements behave exactly as they do on the bench. The
split sections use the real `SplitGrid`.

## 1. Who uses the sheet, and when

**A sveitarforingi on a laptop on Sunday evening.** They're building next Wednesday's fundur.
There's time, a keyboard and a mouse. They:

- pull blocks from the dagskrárbankinn;
- move things around until the evening fits;
- change the lengths;
- open a liður to write notes;
- decide whether Verkefni fundar is one activity or split by flokkur.

**The same person on a phone, at the fundur.** The phone is in one hand, or on a table between two
foringjar, and the room is loud. They glance at it between activities to answer: _what's next,
when does it start, and are we late?_ They rarely edit. If they do, it's a quick fix, such as
moving the game earlier because it's raining.

## 3. What must read from across a room, and what can be secondary

At the meeting, the phone is read at arm's length or from a table. **Readable from across a room**:

1. **The clock.** When each liður starts. The running order _is_ the times.
2. **The title.** What happens.
3. **Whether we're over.** One number, coloured, with the word "yfir". Never colour alone.

**Second read, at normal reading distance:** the duration, a status that isn't "Staðfest", the
endurmat from last time, and the section a liður is in.

**Third read, or only while planning:** the theme, the venue, the cycle week, the kind of
liður, ▲ ▼ ✎ ✕ and the split toggle. These must be present and reachable (rule 8: 44px or more
on touch, never hover-only). They don't need to compete for attention.

### Ranking: the sheet

1. Time range of the evening (19:30–21:10) and the fit (100 / 90 mín · 10 yfir)
2. Title and date
3. What's left to decide (a count, then the liðir)
4. The three sections and their minutes
5. Theme, cycle week, venue, "Öll sveitin"

### Ranking: a row

1. Start time
2. Title
3. Duration
4. Status, when it isn't "Staðfest"; endurmat
5. Actions (▲ ▼ ✎ ✕, grip)
6. Theme and kind

### What's wrong with the current sheet

The current sheet is correct and complete. It reads _evenly_, though:

- The title is the only large thing, and it's set in uppercase, which slows reading.
- Everything else is 12px, grey (`text-tertiary`) and often uppercase.
- The fit, the one number that answers "does this work?", sits in the footer, under the last
  row.
- Every row carries a coloured 3px stripe and an outlined status pill, including "Staðfest". The
  exceptions look the same as the normal case.

None of the directions below use a coloured left stripe or tertiary-grey meta. Secondary text is
`text-secondary`, which passes AA in both themes. A status is a glyph plus a word (✓ Staðfest, ✎
Drög, ~ Óvíst, ? Óákveðið), so colour is never the only signal (WCAG 1.4.1). "Óákveðið" is dashed
and neutral, never red (ADR-002 §3).

## 4. The directions

### A · Dagskrá (running order)

**The story:** "Here's tonight, in order." A printed programme pinned to the wall of the
skátaheimili.

**First read:** the start times (24px, bold, tabular) and the titles (20px). **Second:** the
duration under each time, the evening's range in the header, and the fit in the header and
again in the footer ("Lok kl. 21:10"). **Third:** status, only when it isn't "Staðfest"; the
endurmat, written out as a note under the title; the actions.

**Why it looks this way:**

- Heavy rules above and below the programme, and none of the card chrome, so the sheet reads as
  one document, not a stack of widgets.
- Each section header carries its own start time ("19:40 VERKEFNI FUNDAR"), so you can find your
  place without counting rows.
- "Staðfest" is the expected state at the meeting, so it's left out visually. A screen reader
  still hears it. Only exceptions are drawn, and they stand out because nothing else competes.
- The endurmat is shown in full, not as a link to it. At the meeting, "Of stuttur síðast,
  krakkarnir vildu spila aftur" is exactly the note you need, right when you reach that liður.

**When to use it:** at the meeting, on a phone. It's also the best printout.

**Trade-offs:**

- The large type means fewer rows on screen. A long útilega needs scrolling.
- With "Staðfest" hidden, you can't tell at a glance whether _everything_ is confirmed. You see
  only that nothing is flagged.
- On a phone the actions drop to a second line under each row, so the sheet is taller.
- The design suggests a later "Núna" marker on the current liður. That would need a live clock,
  so it isn't drawn here.

### B · Tímalína (timeline bar)

**The story:** "This is the shape of the evening, and this is where it spills over."

**First read:** the evening bar in the header. It has one segment per liður, sized by its
minutes, and a black line at the planned end ("Áætluð lok 21:00"). Anything past the line is
hatched in the warning colour. **Second:** each row's own bar on the same scale, with the same
line through every row, so the line reads as one rule down the whole sheet. **Third:** the
status tag, endurmat and actions.

**Why it looks this way:**

- Duration is what leaders most often get wrong. A list of equal rows hides it: 45 minutes looks
  like 5. A bar doesn't.
- The current sheet tries to show duration through row _height_. That has to be clamped (5
  minutes still has to be clickable), so the proportion is only partly true. A horizontal bar is
  always exactly proportional, and every row keeps the same height for its controls.
- The overrun is a _shape_: hatched, past a line. "10 yfir" becomes something you see before you
  read it.
- A split section is drawn as one striped block as long as its longest lane. The lanes run side
  by side, so that's when the sveit regroups.
- Bars are coloured by status: confirmed is solid, draft lighter, óvíst amber, and óákveðið a
  dashed outline. The evening bar also shows how settled the evening is.

**When to use it:** planning on a laptop, while fitting the evening to its time. It's still
readable on a phone, where the bars stay full-width.

**Trade-offs:**

- It's the busiest of the four: two bars per liður (header and row).
- The bars are decorative to assistive technology (`aria-hidden`); the same facts are in the text
  ("100 / 90 mín · 10 yfir", each start time and duration).
- Short liðir make thin slivers in the evening bar.
- It's less useful at the meeting, where "what's next" matters more than the shape.

### C · Gátlisti (checklist / readiness)

**The story:** "Here's what's left before Wednesday."

**First read:** the headline, which states only what's outstanding: "2 óákveðnir · 10 mín yfir",
or "Tilbúið: allt ákveðið og passar í tímann" when nothing is. **Second:** a status meter and
legend (Staðfest 3 · Drög 2 · Óvíst 0 · Óákveðið 0), then the "Eftir að ákveða" list. Each entry
in that list is a button that jumps to its row. **Third:** the rows, each led by its status as a
real `<select>`, so it can be changed where you see it.

**Why it looks this way:**

- Planning across a week is a series of small decisions. The sheet should say which ones are
  still open, not make you scan for dashed pills.
- The headline uses the reader's own words ("2 óákveðnir", "10 mín yfir", "Slit tómt"), from
  `countIs`, so the singular and plural agree ("1 óákveðinn").
- The status leads the row because, in this mode, it's the thing you act on. The time and the
  duration step back to the line under the title.

**When to use it:** midweek, on either device, when the question is "am I ready?", not "what's
the order?".

**Trade-offs:**

- The header is tall. On a phone, the first liður is below the fold.
- Status changes are edits (`patch`), and the bench reducer doesn't announce them. The `<select>`
  announces its own new value, but promoting this look should add a reducer announcement.
- The to-do list covers the shared liðir, not the copies in split lanes, which have their own
  grid.

### D · Þétt tafla (compact editor)

**The story:** "Change ten things in a minute."

**First read:** a column of start times and a column of titles. **Second:** a − / + stepper for
the minutes (5-minute steps, announced by the reducer) and a status `<select>` in every row, under
a column head (Kl. · Mín · Liður · Staða). **Third:** ▲ ▼ ✎ ✕, the endurmat flag and the kicker.

**Why it looks this way:**

- On a laptop, the slow part of planning is _opening things_. When the length and status are
  editable in the row, most changes need no editor.
- The rows are about 36px tall on a wide sheet, so a whole fundur fits on screen with room for
  the editor.
- The fit sits at the top right of the header and again in the footer, so you see it change as
  you step the minutes.

**When to use it:** on Sunday evening at a laptop, as the default editing view. It's the worst
fit for the meeting itself.

**Trade-offs:**

- It's dense and small (14px), which makes it the least readable from a distance.
- On a phone each row stacks into three lines (title, then stepper and status, then actions), to
  keep 48px targets. That works, but it's the least elegant of the four on a small screen.
- The column head is visual only (`aria-hidden`); each control names its liður itself.

These directions combine naturally. One option is **D for editing and A for reading**, one
sheet with a "Lesa / Breyta" switch, with **C's headline** and **B's evening bar** in the header
of both. That would give the leader both answers before the first row: does it fit, and what's
left.

## 6. What every direction keeps

- The three sections (Setning, Verkefni fundar, Slit), with an empty section stated, not hidden.
- Start times derived from the durations (`startTimes`). On an undated fundur they become offsets
  from the start (`+0:40`), so a scratchpad still has a running order.
- Status, endurmat, ▲ ▼, the grip, ✎, ✕, and "Skipta á flokka" / "Sameina í eitt".
- The keyboard path: the row is the keyboard surface. Space grabs, ↑ ↓ move while grabbed (and
  move focus otherwise), Enter opens the editor, Delete removes, Escape lets go. Focus follows a
  grabbed row across a move, and every move is announced through the bench's live regions.
- 48px targets on a narrow sheet or a touch screen (`--sl-size-button-lg`); 32px with a mouse on a
  wide sheet. Nothing is revealed only on hover.
- Light and dark from `--sl-*` tokens only. Counts go through `countIs`, dates through
  `formatIcelandicDayMonth`. The only motion (row background) collapses under
  `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Files

`stories/lab/fundur-sheet/`:

- `FundurSheetLab.tsx`: the sheet, with a `look` prop.
- `LidurRowLab.tsx`: the row, with a `look` prop.
- `sheetModel.ts`: the shared facts (fit, undecided, offsets) and copy.
- `labHarness.tsx`: live bench state, focus handling and the announcer.
- `FundurSheetLab.module.css`: the styles.
- `FundurSheetLab.stories.tsx`: the stories.

Each direction has a "reorder" story whose play test reorders with ▲ and checks that the start times follow, then puts
the liður back with the keyboard.
